1. Bending: the neutral axis, and y

2. Three quantities, one letter

NameSymbol & unitsRound sectionWhere it appears
Second moment of areaI   m⁴ π·d⁴/64 Bending stress σ = M·y/I and deflection. Pure geometry — resistance to bending.
Polar second moment of areaJ   m⁴ π·d⁴/32 = 2I Torsional shear τ = T·r/J. Pure geometry — resistance to twist. Circular sections only.
Mass moment of inertiaI   kg·m² m·r²/2 Rotor energy ½·I·ω² and angular acceleration. Involves mass. Never in M·y/I or T·r/J.
The units are the check.

m⁴ and kg·m² are not the same kind of thing. If something in m⁴ turns up inside an energy calculation, or something in kg·m² turns up inside a stress calculation, stop — you have picked up the wrong quantity, and no amount of careful arithmetic afterwards will fix it.

For an 8 mm solid shaft: I = 2.011 × 10⁻¹⁰ m⁴, J = 4.021 × 10⁻¹⁰ m⁴. The DP-2 screen's rotor mass moment of inertia is a different number in different units entirely, and it is what produces the 5.087 kJ of stored energy quoted in the review.

3. What sizes this shaft — and which baseline it came from

A config comment is not a material certificate.

tau_allow_shaft_Pa: 280.0e6 # 4340 steel, allowable shear is a number chosen so the model runs. It names no temper, no heat treatment, no temperature, no surface finish, no fatigue basis and no factor of safety. Quoting it back does not turn it into an allowable — and an ultimate tensile strength, or a room-temperature handbook value, is not one either.

Three bearing bores, three design points.

config/seed.yaml carries d_bearing_bore_m: 0.015 and is still the earlier 250 N / PR 3.2 baseline. DP-2 proposes an 8 mm journal, which is where docs/project/dp2-review.md gets 8 mm × 66,000 rpm = 528,000 DN. The PD-1 candidate now on main uses 10 mm journals, set by the 9.99 mm bore of the candidate JETMAX TW85 turbine wheel, with a 68,000 rpm design point (config/cases/pd1-jm85.yaml, docs/design/design-basis.md). Taking one design point’s power and another’s geometry produces a number that is true of none of them — and the review says explicitly that existing baseline results cannot be presented as DP-2 results.

Stress margin does not buy speed margin.

docs/model-review.md reports a preliminary bending critical speed near 23,400 rpm for the earlier baseline, with startup crossing it and about 10% separation from the proposed continuous range against the 20% criterion in config/limits.yaml. That is a stiffness-and-mass problem. Those baseline numbers are not DP-2 results and must not be presented as such.

Everything here is elastic, static, plain-section screening. Stress concentration at steps, keyways, circlip grooves and cross-holes; fatigue; combined bending and torsion; buckling; and temperature-dependent properties are all outside it. Finishing this lesson approves nothing.
