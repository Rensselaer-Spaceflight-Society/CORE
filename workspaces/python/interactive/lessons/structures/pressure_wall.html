2. The same formula, two completely different jobs

Reading the plot.

Stress goes as 1/t, so halving the wall doubles the stress — a straight line of slope −1 on log–log axes. That also means thickness buys less and less: 1.5 mm to 3 mm halves the membrane stress, but 3 mm to 4.5 mm takes off only another third, while mass climbs and the sheet gets harder to roll and to weld without distortion.

What this screen does not know.

Buckling under net external pressure or axial compression. Stress concentration at every hole, boss and penetration. Welds and the heat-affected zone beside them — and a girth joint carries only half the hoop stress, so the weak direction is not always the obvious one. Properties that fall with temperature. Fatigue over start–run–shutdown cycles. Fragment containment, which this has nothing whatever to do with.

A small number here closes none of those. It means only that the membrane term is small.

3. Gas temperature is not wall temperature

The DP-2 candidate turbine inlet total temperature is about 1150 K. That number describes gas. The metal sits somewhere else entirely, set by what heats it, what cools it and what it conducts into. In a reverse-flow layout the outer casing is on the cold side of the liner and is washed by compressor delivery air — much cooler than the flame, but “much cooler” is not a temperature, and a material allowable cannot be chosen from an adjective.

docs/model-review.md lists temperature-dependent material allowables as an open assumption, and docs/project/dp2-review.md item 7 makes the same point for the turbine: needing metal-temperature evidence is not the same as having permission to infer an allowable from gas temperature.
