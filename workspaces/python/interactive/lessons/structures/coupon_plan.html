5. Filler, and the strength that is not there

A 6061-T6 butt weld fails in the heat-affected zone whichever filler you use.

So parent-metal T6 properties are not the joint's properties, and quoting them for a welded assembly overstates it. Any allowable for a welded joint has to be a welded allowable, from a design source, at the service temperature and for the loading direction.

Trade guidance (F. Armao, The Welder, 3 Oct 2006 — guidance, not a specification): 5356 has roughly 50% higher shear strength, which matters for lap and fillet joints loaded in shear, and anodises silver to match the parent. 4043 is less crack-sensitive, shows less crater cracking, is preferred for service above about 150 °F, and anodises black. Both are stronger than the HAZ in a butt joint, which is why that choice turns on shear loading, cracking risk and service temperature rather than on tensile strength.

6. Money: count a donation once

  • Cash is price plus shipping, one supplier, one quantity, one currency, with a date. It excludes tax, duty, tooling, consumables and shop time.
  • Confirmed in-kind is a separate line: what, confirmed by whom, arriving when. The lesson does not subtract it from the cash figure, so that the Chief engineer can credit it exactly once against the line it actually replaces.
  • Free machine-shop access already exists — it is not a new saving to subtract a second time. Atlas Copco sponsorship is confirmed as a relationship; its scope, value and delivery date are NOT FOUND, so nothing is credited against it yet.
  • An unknown price is UNKNOWN, never 0. A false zero is worse than a visible gap.
